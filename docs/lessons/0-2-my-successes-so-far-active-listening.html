0.2.1 – My successes and qualities

👤 MY SUCCESSES SO FAR – INDIVIDUAL

Write, draw, or think quietly for 2-3 minutes — afterwards, we will ask you to share some of your ideas with another person!

📝

“My successes for far…”

  • What is one success or achievement you are proud of in your life or enterprise?
  • It can be big or small!
  • What qualities do you like and value most about yourself?
  • Try to write 3+ qualities if you can!
  • For example: “I am a good listener; I make high quality products; I’m Honest; Respectful; etc.”

0.2.2 – Active Listening

☝️ INTRODUCE ACTIVE LISTENING

Connect this activity to the Community Agreement:

We have agreed to respect and support one another during this training. One way we can practice that agreement is by listening carefully when another person is speaking.

Introduce active listening:

Active listening means listening with your full attention and effort to understand. It helps you understand the person who is speaking, and it helps that person feel supported.

Sometimes, while another person is speaking, our minds wander or we begin thinking about what we will say next. When we listen actively, we bring our attention back to the speaker.”

Active listening is a useful skill for an entrepreneur and a leader. It can help us understand our customers, business partners, family members, and other people in our community.

Explain the three active-listening tips that participants will practice:

  • Give your full attention when someone is speaking to you - try not to interrupt.

  • Listen for the emotions (think-feel-do cycles) behind the words, and try to be calm and understanding even if you disagree with what is being said.

  • When it is your turn to speak, begin by repeating back what you have heard (“What I heard you say was…”)

Prepare participants for the combined activity:

You do not need to remember every detail. Today, listen for one success the person is proud of and one quality that helped the person succeed.

👤 PAIR SHARING & ACTIVE LISTENING PRACTICE

Ask participants to form pairs with someone they do not know well yet to share what they wrote to the questions. In each pair, “Person A” is the speaker, and “Person B” is the listener. Then they switch. Have participants spread out around the room so that they do not interrupt or distract each other. You can have participants go outside, as long as you can still monitor that they are participating. Go through Part I and II below. Give the following instructions: PART I Person A — Speaker: Share one success or achievement you are proud of. Then share one or more qualities that helped you achieve it. Person B — Listener: Give the speaker your full attention. Try not to interrupt. Listen for the success and the qualities the speaker describes. The listener does not need to ask questions, give advice, or remember every detail. PART II Person B — Listener: After Person A speaks for three minutes, Person B’s job is to repeat back to Person A what she heard. Start by saying, “What I heard you say was…” Person B should not share her thoughts. She should only repeat back what she heard. Person A — Speaker: If Person B misheard, Person A can correct them so that they understand exactly what was meant.

After approximately four minutes, announce: Please switch roles. Person B is now the speaker, and Person A is now the listener.

Your response

💬 GROUP DISCUSSION

Bring the group back together.

Invite two or three brief responses:

  • How did sharing your successes and qualities make you feel?
  • How did you feel when your partner listened carefully and repeated back what she heard?
  • What helped you listen carefully when your partner was speaking?

Thank participants:

“Thank you for sharing and for celebrating the strengths and courage that you heard from one another.”

Team notes & insights

🔑 CLOSING KEY MESSAGES

  • We often see positive qualities in other people, but not in ourselves.
  • By taking time to identify our qualities, we will start to see them more often and feel more confident to overcome the challenges that come our way.
  • Active listening means listening with your full attention and effort to understand.
  • We can practice active listening with customers and business partners, as well as with family members, friends, and other people in our community.
  • We will continue practicing active listening throughout this program whenever we share our thoughts and experiences with one another.
